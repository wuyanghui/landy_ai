# Rainwater Harvesting System (SPAH)

**SPAH** (Sistem Penuaian Air Hujan) is the state-mandated rainwater harvesting standard set out in Section AS6 of the [[summaries/PEQ-alam-sekitar]] guideline, governing how buildings capture, store, and reuse rainwater to reduce potable water demand and support sustainable water management across Selangor.

## Scope of Application

SPAH installation is **mandatory for all building categories with a roof catchment area of 100m² or more**. This threshold applies broadly across residential, commercial, and institutional development, making rainwater harvesting a near-universal requirement for medium and large buildings rather than an optional green feature.

## Core System Elements

Every SPAH installation consists of four main elements:

1. **Catchment area** — the roof surface collecting rainwater (roof type affects water quality and collection efficiency)
2. **Drainage/gutter system** — gutters and downpipes conveying water from roof to storage
3. **Storage tank** — holding the harvested water, sited above ground, underground, or on rooftops/above the building
4. **Distribution system** — delivering stored water to points of use

Ten SPAH components are specified in detail: catchment area, gutter, first flush device, conveyance piping, filtration (pre-filtration via leaf filters/basket strainers, plus finer filtration), storage tank, pump, part-fill valve, and inlet/outlet submeters.

## Installation Methods

Three installation methods are permitted, each with detailed plumbing schematics:

- **Gravity Fed System** — relies on gravity to move water from an elevated tank to points of use, without a pump
- **Direct Pumping System** — pumps water directly from storage to distribution points
- **Indirect Pumping System** — pumps water into an intermediate domestic water tank before distribution

Each method's diagrams show the relationship between the rainwater tank, domestic water tank, first flush device, gutter, downpipe, and pump connections.

## Sizing and Design Specifications

Storage tank capacity is scaled to development size (small/medium/large) and dwelling type (single, semi-detached, terrace), governed by the sizing formula:

> **S = 0.01 × A**

where S is storage capacity and A is catchment area, with separate requirements distinguishing residential from non-residential buildings.

## Landscape-Based Components

Beyond the building-level system, SPAH incorporates landscape-based stormwater management components that complement [[concepts/buffer-zone-requirements]] and drainage planning:

- In-situ detention/retention ponds (dry and wet detention ponds)
- Swales
- Bermed landscape
- French drains
- On-site detention (OSD) for car parks and recreational plots

These elements extend rainwater management beyond individual buildings into the broader site and landscape design, echoing the flood-mitigation and water-catchment concerns raised elsewhere in the guideline for [[concepts/river-reserve-development]] and environmentally sensitive areas ([[concepts/kawasan-sensitif-alam-sekitar-ksas]]).

## Approval and Compliance

Building plan submissions must include SPAH design details — gutter/downpipe layout, first flush device, storage tank, and distribution system — as a condition of approval. Building owners are also required to include SPAH maintenance in their building maintenance schedules in accordance with MS ISO 9001:2008.

## Significance

SPAH reflects the guideline's broader emphasis on water conservation and sustainable development, connecting directly to the water-related Sustainable Development Goals referenced in Bahagian A of [[summaries/PEQ-alam-sekitar]] and to national green building/water efficiency trends cited as best practice examples (e.g., residential, office, and industrial rainwater harvesting installations).
