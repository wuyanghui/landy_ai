# Inclusive and Accessible Housing Design

## Overview

Inclusive and accessible housing design is a recurring planning requirement threaded through the *Garis Panduan dan Piawaian Perancangan Perumahan Negeri Selangor, 2025* ([[summaries/PEQ-Perumahan]]), published by [[entities/planmalaysia-selangor]]. Rather than being confined to a single section, provisions for OKU (persons with disabilities), the elderly (warga emas), and children are embedded across nearly every housing typology's technical standards — from landed terrace and detached houses through to strata cluster schemes, condominiums, and affordable Rumah Selangorku developments.

## Origins in the Guideline's Design Principles

The document's foundational planning principles (Bahagian A, Seksyen 5.1) explicitly call for housing environments that are comfortable, conducive, and safe for all user groups, and for planning that fosters social cohesion and positive community interaction. Section 2.1 (Issues and Challenges) identifies the historical **lack of consideration for OKU, the elderly, and children in planning** as one of six key problems the 2025 guideline is designed to correct, marking accessibility as a deliberate corrective priority rather than an afterthought.

## Application Across Housing Typologies

- **Rumah Kluster Berkembar (PR5)**: cluster housing is explicitly designed with shared communal/interaction spaces intended to serve the elderly and children alongside general residents.
- **Perumahan Strata Bertanah (PR4)**: communal facility sizing formulas account for OKU-accessible parking and reference OKU design standards directly.
- **Pangsapuri/Kondominium (PR6)**: standards mandate provisions for OKU, elderly, and childcare (tadika/taska) units, dedicated elderly activity spaces sized by unit count, and OKU-designated parking bays alongside EV charging bay (EVCB) provisions.
- **[[entities/rumah-selangorku]] (PR7)**: affordable housing schemes carry the same inclusivity requirements — OKU and elderly-friendly unit design, elderly activity spaces with unit-count-based sizing tables, childcare facilities, and OKU/tandem parking ratios — extending accessible design commitments to the lower-cost segment of the market rather than treating it as a premium feature.

## Relationship to Parking and Facility Standards

OKU accessibility is operationalized concretely through parking ratio requirements (dedicated OKU bays, often paired with EV charging bay provisions) and through sizing formulas for communal facilities — elderly activity rooms, childcare centers, prayer rooms, and multipurpose halls — that scale with the number of housing units in a scheme. This ties inclusive design directly into the broader [[concepts/affordable-housing-provision-standards]] framework, since these facility and parking requirements apply across both market-rate and Rumah Selangorku affordable schemes.

## Strategic Context

Inclusive design is also positioned as one pillar supporting the state's livability agenda under [[entities/bandar-berdaya-huni-negeri-selangor]] (Growth, Livability, Sustainability, Resiliency), reflecting a shift toward planning that treats accessibility and multigenerational usability as core measures of a well-designed housing environment rather than a compliance add-on.
