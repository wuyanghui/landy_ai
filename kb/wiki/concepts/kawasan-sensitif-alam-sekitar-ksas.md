# Kawasan Sensitif Alam Sekitar (KSAS)

## Definition

Kawasan Sensitif Alam Sekitar (KSAS), or Environmentally Sensitive Area, is the core classification concept underpinning [[summaries/PEQ-alam-sekitar]], the 2025 Selangor state environmental planning guideline. KSAS designates land where environmental characteristics — slope, geology, hydrology, ecological value, or heritage value — impose special restrictions or conditions on development. The guideline's stated purpose is to help local authorities (PBT) and Selangor state agencies manage the environmental impact of development based on KSAS criteria identified within Structure Plans and Local Plans/Special Area Plans (RT/RKK).

## Legal and Policy Basis

KSAS as a planning instrument is anchored in the Selangor State Structure Plan ([[entities/rsn-selangor-2035]]), which sets the strategic direction for identifying environmentally sensitive zones, coastal areas, and integrated river basin management. It is further reinforced by national frameworks referenced in the guideline's policy chapter, including the [[entities/sustainable-development-goals]], the 12th Malaysia Plan ([[entities/rmk12]]), the Fourth National Physical Plan ([[entities/rfn4]]), the Second National Urbanisation Policy ([[entities/dpn2]]), and the National Policy on the Environment ([[entities/dasn]]). Enforcement and technical assessment involve multiple agencies, including [[entities/jabatan-mineral-dan-geosains-jmg]] (geology), [[entities/jabatan-pengairan-dan-saliran-jps]] and [[entities/lembaga-urus-air-selangor-luas]] (water resources), and [[entities/jabatan-alam-sekitar-jas]] (environmental compliance).

## How KSAS Is Applied Across the Guideline

Rather than a single standard, KSAS functions as an umbrella sensitivity classification applied across each of the special technical guidelines (AS1–AS6) in Bahagian B of the document:

- **Hillside and highland areas (AS1-1)** — Slope terrain is classified into four classes (Kelas I–IV) based on gradient, geotechnical characteristics, and landslide risk, cross-referenced against elevation bands (below 150m, 150–300m, 300m–1,000m, above 1,000m). Higher classes and elevations trigger stricter controls, referral to the JKTPKSAS technical committee, and mandatory [[concepts/geological-site-clearance-bpgt]] reporting. Peat and soft soil areas are separately assessed (AS1-2) for thickness and disaster risk.
- **Lakes (AS2)** — Natural and man-made lakes are zoned into littoral, ecozone, and catchment areas, with permitted uses and [[concepts/buffer-zone-requirements]] varying by lake type and zone.
- **Trees (AS3)** — Tree Preservation Orders under [[entities/akta-172]] protect historic, rare, endemic, and endangered trees within KSAS-relevant catchment, hill, and forest reserve areas.
- **Coastal zones (AS4)** — Beachfront land is categorized by heritage, life-support, and disaster-risk value, with setback distances from the mean high water spring line varying by beach type and proposed use.
- **River reserves (AS5)** — Development along river corridors is governed by [[concepts/river-reserve-development]], which sets reserve widths, control zones, and permitted/prohibited waterfront structures to protect river ecosystems.
- **Water efficiency (AS6)** — While not a direct KSAS zoning tool, mandatory rainwater harvesting ([[concepts/rainwater-harvesting-spah]]) supports the guideline's broader environmental sensitivity and resource conservation goals.

## Underlying Rationale

The guideline frames KSAS designation as a response to five recurring development issues: weak proactive planning ahead of development pressure, high development pressure against limited developable land, individual/party encroachment and negligence in sensitive zones, uncontrolled development concepts affecting hills, riverbanks, forests, and coastlines, and weak enforcement of relocation and environmental provisions. KSAS classification is the mechanism intended to make these risks visible and actionable within the planning approval process, requiring geotechnical, hydrological, or ecological assessment before development in flagged zones.
