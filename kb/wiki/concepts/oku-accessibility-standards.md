# OKU (Disabled) Accessibility Standards

OKU (Orang Kurang Upaya) accessibility standards are a mandatory, cross-cutting requirement embedded throughout Selangor's planning guidelines — recurring as a standard requirement in every one of the twenty commercial development categories (PN1–PN20) covered in [[summaries/PEQ-Perniagaan]], as a dedicated technical standard (TLK5) with detailed dimensional specifications in [[summaries/PEQ-infrastruktur]], and as a near-universal site/parking requirement across all nine community facility categories (KM1–KM9) in [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]]. Rather than being addressed as a standalone chapter, these requirements recur systematically across site planning, parking, building access, and support facilities — reflecting the shared planning principle of "innovative, distinctive identity in design" that explicitly includes OKU-friendly universal design, the infrastructure guideline's Universal Design (UD) provisions under PL4 (Piawaian Perancangan Jalan), and the community facilities guideline's own basic planning principle of "Keselesaan dan Keselamatan" (comfort and safety), which explicitly references Universal Design Guidelines alongside [[concepts/cpted-crime-prevention-through-environmental-design]].

## Core Requirements

- **Compliance baseline**: All developments must comply with [[entities/ms1184]], the Malaysian Standard governing universal accessibility in the built environment. This standard is cited as the reference baseline in nearly every PN chapter of [[summaries/PEQ-Perniagaan]], from terrace shophouses (PN1) to sports complexes (PN20), and is referenced directly in [[summaries/PEQ-infrastruktur]] for OKU parking bay signage (MS 1184:2014) and pedestrian resting-area design.
- **Dedicated OKU parking bays**: A consistent minimum quota of 2% of total car parking bays is reserved for OKU users across most development types (e.g., terrace shops, business complexes, hotels, hospitals, MICE centres, sports complexes), typically sited near lift access with ramps and railings. Some smaller-format developments (e.g., affordable terrace shops, bazaars) specify a minimum of 1 unit regardless of percentage. The same 2% quota (or an equivalent fixed-ratio allocation) recurs throughout [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]], applied to hospitals and health clinics (KM1), primary/secondary schools and IPTA campuses (KM2), police headquarters, stations and posts and fire and rescue stations (KM3), mosques, surau and non-Islamic places of worship (KM4), community complexes, centres, and children's/elderly activity and rehabilitation centres (KM6), public libraries (KM7), public markets (KM8), and public transport terminals (KM9), where it is paired with a minimum of 2 dedicated ambulance bays. The infrastructure guideline's TLK5 standard sets the precise dimensions for these bays: 5.40m x 3.60m with a 1.20m wheelchair transfer zone, marked with a wheelchair symbol (1040mm x 900mm), located near main entrances with clear signage and level, non-slip surfacing.
- **Physical access features**: Ramps, railings, and barrier-free walkways are required at building entrances, parking-to-lift routes, and pedestrian circulation paths, ensuring continuous accessible movement from arrival to building interior. PL4's Universal Design standards specify a maximum entrance ramp gradient of 1:20 with minimum landing widths, step-ramp connections from OKU bays to building entrances of at least 1.00m width, tactile block guidance strips and warning strips along pedestrian walkways, and obstacle-free walkway zones. Resting areas and benches along accessible routes follow MS1184:2014 dimensional standards. [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]] echoes this through its own accessibility principle (Kemudahsampaian), which requires connected pedestrian/cycling paths for OKU users within roughly 400m/5-minute-walk catchments, and through facility-specific provisions such as mandatory lifts for vertical (5-storey-and-above) school and community complex developments and for special-needs schools (sekolah pendidikan khas) exceeding 2 storeys, dedicated ramps and separated pedestrian/logistics access for strata-based tadika/taska, and disabled/motorcycle circulation bays within burial ground layouts (KM5).
- **OKU-accessible EV charging**: [[concepts/ev-charging-bay-requirements]] standards intersect directly with OKU provisions — several PN chapters (e.g., PN10 Hentian Rehat dan Rawat, PN20 Kompleks Sukan) specify OKU-accessible electric vehicle charging bays as a distinct sub-requirement, and the infrastructure guideline's EVCB standards (TLK7) similarly carve out OKU/disabled EV bay quotas alongside motorcycle EV bay allocations. [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]] applies the same pairing at safety facilities (police headquarters, stations, and posts) and fire and rescue stations, where EV charging bay provisions are specified alongside OKU parking ratios.
- **Sanitary and welfare facilities**: Public toilets are required to include separated OKU-accessible facilities alongside male/female divisions, seen in hotel, hospital, RnR, and food-centre (medan selera) standards, and similarly in the community facilities guideline's public market (Pasar Awam) and community centre component lists.
- **Accessible payment and controls**: Where OKU parking bays include ticketing or payment machines, these must be mounted between 800mm and 1100mm in height to remain reachable from a seated position, per TLK5.
- **OKU as a served population, not just a design constraint**: Beyond physical accessibility, [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]]'s community facilities chapter (KM6-2) establishes dedicated welfare institutions serving OKU residents directly — Pusat Pemulihan Dalam Komuniti (community-based rehabilitation centres) operate under formal OKU referral and registration procedures coordinated with the Department of Social Welfare, alongside children's and elderly activity centres, reflecting a facility-provision role for OKU communities that goes beyond parking and ramp standards.
- **National urban policy grounding**: [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]] situates OKU accessibility within Dasar Perbandaran Negara 2's Prinsip 4 (Inclusive & Equitable Urban Development), illustrating the policy with OKU-friendly infrastructure examples, reinforcing that accessibility is a state-level urban policy commitment rather than a facility-specific afterthought.

## Where OKU Standards Appear

OKU provisions are documented in nearly every PN chapter of [[summaries/PEQ-Perniagaan]], throughout the TLK5/PL4/TLK7 standards of [[summaries/PEQ-infrastruktur]], and across all nine facility categories (KM1 Kemudahan Kesihatan, KM2 Kemudahan Pendidikan, KM3 Kemudahan Keselamatan, KM4 Kemudahan Keagamaan, KM5 Kemudahan Perkuburan, KM6 Kemudahan Komuniti, KM7 Tanah Lapang dan Rekreasi, KM8 Pasar Awam, and KM9 Terminal/Stesen Pengangkutan Awam) of [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]].
