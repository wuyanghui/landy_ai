# Transit-Oriented Development (TOD)

Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) is a state planning policy that concentrates housing density, mixed land uses, and walkable/cyclable access around public transit stations, aiming to reduce private vehicle dependency and integrate residential, commercial, and community-facility development with LRT/MRT infrastructure. It functions as a cross-cutting siting principle applied across Selangor's housing ([[summaries/PEQ-Perumahan]]), business/commercial ([[summaries/PEQ-Perniagaan]]), and community facilities ([[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]]) planning guidelines alike.

## Policy Basis

TOD is formalized under [[entities/dasar-tod-selangor]] (Dasar Perancangan Pembangunan Berorientasikan Transit Negeri Selangor), first established and gazetted in 2016. It sets out nine core principles for transit-oriented, mixed-use, walkable, densified development within a defined catchment radius of transit stations, covering mixed-use development, density bonuses, affordable housing and commercial unit provision, pedestrian/cycling paths, parking management, feeder bus services, and green building promotion. This state policy is part of the broader planning policy stack alongside [[entities/rmk12]], [[entities/rfn4]], [[entities/dpn2]], and [[entities/rsn-selangor-2035]], and supports the livability goals of [[entities/bandar-berdaya-huni-negeri-selangor]] (Growth, Livability, Sustainability, Resiliency).

## Role in Housing Planning

TOD status directly affects affordable housing quotas: the housing guideline's zoning framework distinguishes TOD/TAD/CBD areas from non-TOD areas when setting mandatory affordable-housing provision percentages by zone and land size (see [[concepts/affordable-housing-provision-standards]]). Developments within TOD-designated zones near transit stations face different (generally higher) affordable housing quota requirements than those in ordinary urban or fringe zones, reflecting the policy intent to concentrate density — including [[entities/rumah-selangorku]] affordable housing — around transit infrastructure.

## Role in Commercial and Business Planning

The business/commercial guideline treats transit proximity as a core siting principle: Section 6.0's general standards for site and location planning require strategic, accessible sites near CBDs and transit stations (LRT/MRT/bus terminals), with development integrated into surrounding land use rather than sited in isolation. The guideline's best-practice initiatives section further identifies "Transportation Infrastructure and Economic Corridors" — highways, MRT lines, and ERL connections linking business and industrial areas — as a key trend shaping where commercial developments locate, alongside "Integrated Commercial Zones" that combine office, retail, residential, and recreational space in strategic, transit-accessible locations. This extends TOD's influence beyond housing density into commercial site selection, parking demand management, and mixed-use complex design (e.g., business complexes, MICE centres, hypermarkets).

## Role in Community Facilities Planning

The community facilities guideline (Garis Panduan dan Piawaian Perancangan Kemudahan Masyarakat, 2025) embeds transit accessibility directly into its siting criteria. Under KM2 (Kemudahan Pendidikan), primary and secondary school site selection explicitly considers proximity to public transport — within roughly 400m of catchment residential areas — alongside walking-distance requirements from surrounding neighbourhoods. Most directly, KM9 (Terminal/Stesen Pengangkutan Awam) sets out a full hierarchy of transit terminal types — Integrated Terminal (50 acres), District Terminal (25 acres), Public Transport Hub Terminal (12.5 acres), Support/Feeder Terminal (4 acres), and bus stops/shelters — and requires that these terminals be sited within or near CBDs, integrated with rail stations, located near institutions (universities, colleges), and coordinated with surrounding residential redevelopment. Its stated development concept of "integrated transport modes" (rail, bus, ferry, taxi) operationalizes TOD principles at the facility-planning level, requiring terminals to integrate with pedestrian movement and adjoining land use rather than function as standalone infrastructure. TOD-aligned densification also underpins the guideline's [[concepts/vertical-facility-development]] models for schools and community complexes, which offer compact high-rise alternatives to conventional low-rise layouts in land-constrained, transit-accessible urban areas.

## Best Practice Integration

Across the housing, commercial, and community facilities guidelines, TOD-integrated development is listed as a key best-practice initiative for Selangor. In housing, this emphasizes pedestrian and cycling connectivity between housing schemes and LRT/MRT stations, alongside sustainability and smart-infrastructure goals, influencing internal road layout, walkway provision, and access standards in the housing typology guidelines (PR1–PR7). In the commercial guideline, TOD principles surface alongside Digital Smart City Development (5G/IoT infrastructure) and Economic Corridor development. In the community facilities guideline, transit accessibility functions as a shared siting criterion feeding into the broader [[concepts/population-based-facility-hierarchy]] used to size and locate schools, community complexes, and transport terminals alike — reinforcing transit accessibility as a common design and siting principle across development types, all governed by [[entities/planmalaysia-selangor]].
