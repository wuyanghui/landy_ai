# Vertical/High-Rise Facility Development

## Definition

Vertical (menegak) facility development is a design approach permitting community facilities to be built as multi-storey, compact structures rather than low-rise horizontal layouts, primarily to conserve land in urban and land-constrained areas. It is offered as an explicit alternative to conventional horizontal development across several facility categories in the [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]] guideline, reflecting Selangor's broader densification and [[concepts/transit-oriented-development]] direction.

## Where It Applies

- **Schools (KM2)**: Both primary and secondary schools offer a "5 Tingkat ke Atas" (5 storeys and above) vertical model alongside the conventional 4-storey-and-below horizontal model. Vertical schools require site-suitability assessment and design criteria such as skybridges, atriums, informal learning spaces, seamless indoor-outdoor circulation, and lift ratios calculated per floor. Special-needs schools (sekolah pendidikan khas) require lifts once they exceed 2 storeys.
- **Integrated School Complexes (KM2-3)**: Provide parallel specifications for a 4-storey-and-below "Konvensional" model and a 5-storey-and-above "Menegak" model, each with distinct road width, parking, and recreation-area sizing rules.
- **Community Complexes/Centres (KM6-1)**: Kompleks Komuniti/Pusat Komuniti developments can follow horizontal (max 3 storeys), clustered, or vertical/high-rise integrated forms. Vertical integrated development (up to 4 storeys) stacks multiple facility types by floor level — e.g., taska/nursery, clinic, library, police post, welfare facilities, hall, and sports court within a single building, exemplified by Kompleks Komuniti Menegak (UTC Selangor).
- **Hospitals and IPTA campuses**: Site area notes for hospitals and public higher-education institutions similarly encourage vertical/compact design to minimize land footprint where site area is constrained.

## Rationale

Vertical development responds to two of the guideline's stated challenges: uneven facility distribution in high-density areas and the mismatch between facility provision and population growth (see [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]] background section). By stacking multiple facility functions within one building footprint, it also supports the guideline's principle of **Integrasi Kemudahan** (facility integration) and its Industry 4.0/technology-alignment principle.

## Design and Planning Considerations

- Vertical models generally carry adjusted (often reduced) minimum site-area requirements compared to horizontal equivalents, since floor area is achieved through building height rather than land coverage.
- Circulation elements specific to vertical design — lifts, atriums, skybridges — are mandated in addition to standard [[concepts/parking-standards]], [[concepts/oku-accessibility-standards]], and [[concepts/road-hierarchy-selangor]] requirements that apply to both models.
- Recreation and open-space allocations (e.g., playing fields) may be reduced or reconfigured (podium-level, roof gardens) for vertical/stacked facilities, as seen in the KM(1) general standards on podium-level recreation limits.
- Vertical integrated developments require explicit floor-by-floor allocation planning to coordinate co-located facility types with differing access, security, and service needs.

## Related Concepts

- [[concepts/transit-oriented-development]] — vertical facility development complements Selangor's TOD policy by concentrating services near transit nodes.
- [[concepts/population-based-facility-hierarchy]] — the population catchment tier often determines whether a horizontal or vertical model is applicable.
- [[concepts/parking-standards]] and [[concepts/oku-accessibility-standards]] — apply across both development models but require adaptation (e.g., lift-based OKU access) in vertical designs.
