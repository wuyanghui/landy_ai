# Worker Welfare Facilities

## Overview

Worker welfare facilities encompass the housing, amenity, and safety provisions that industrial developments in Selangor must supply for their workforce, as set out across [[summaries/PEQ-Perindustrian]]. These requirements appear both as general, cross-cutting standards in Bahagian A and as a dedicated typology (IND6) in Bahagian B, reflecting the guideline's recognition that industrial zones cannot be planned around production alone — the people who work in them need housing, sustenance, prayer space, and safety infrastructure.

## Legal Basis

Worker dormitory (Asrama Pekerja) provisions are governed by [[entities/act-446]] (Workers' Minimum Standards of Housing and Amenities Act 1990), which sets minimum conditions for worker accommodation. This statutory foundation applies across the general standards and is reinforced by the Housing and Centralised Labour Quarters Standards Regulations 2020 for larger centralised facilities.

## General Standards (Bahagian A)

Across all industrial typologies, the guideline mandates baseline supporting facilities:

- **Prayer rooms (surau)** — sized by formula based on worker headcount, including ablution area dimensions
- **Canteens and rest areas** — for worker meals and breaks
- **Worker dormitories** — subject to building setback rules, capped at a maximum of 15% of plot gross floor area, with safety and gender-segregation rules
- **Toilets, management offices, and staff transport** considerations

## Type-Specific Requirements

Worker welfare facilities recur throughout the type-specific chapters of Bahagian B:

- **IND1–IND4** (terrace, detached/cluster, landed strata, and stratified multi-storey industrial) each specify canteen, prayer room, and dormitory provisions scaled to the building type, alongside warehouse and loading facilities.
- **IND6 — Penginapan Pekerja Berpusat (Centralised Labour Quarters)** is a dedicated typology triggered when an industrial premise houses 500 or more workers. It requires:
  - Separate male/female dormitory blocks
  - Shared amenities: kitchen/dining, laundry, sick bay/quarantine room, management office
  - Safety infrastructure: guard house, security fencing, CCTV, biometric access, fire safety per JBPM (Fire and Rescue Department) requirements
  - Recreational facilities: futsal, badminton, sepak takraw, and volleyball courts, plus a recreation room
  - Optional convenience facilities: mini-mart, barber, clinic, ATM
  - Dedicated circulation: entry/exit access, emergency access, and pick-up/drop-off lay-by zones

  CLQ developments above certain thresholds also trigger environmental review (SIA Category B, or EIA review by the Department of Environment for light industries).

## Policy Linkage

Worker housing is not only a planning-standard concern but a stated policy objective: [[entities/dpn2]] (Second National Urbanisation Policy) explicitly targets affordable housing for industrial workers as part of its urbanisation strategy, situating these facility standards within a broader national push for inclusive industrial growth.

## Relation to Other Concepts

Worker welfare facilities intersect with [[concepts/sustainable-industrial-planning]], since CLQ site layouts incorporate the same green landscaping and buffer principles applied elsewhere, and with [[concepts/buffer-zone-requirements]], as dormitory blocks must maintain setback distances from industrial operations and boundaries for safety.
