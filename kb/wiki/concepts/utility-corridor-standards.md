# Utility Corridor Standards

## Overview

Utility corridors (Koridor Utiliti) are shared underground and roadside lanes designated for laying essential services — electrical cable, telecommunications cable, water pipe, sewerage pipe, and gas pipe — in a coordinated manner alongside road infrastructure. The standard (KU1: Laluan Koridor Utiliti) is set out in [[summaries/PEQ-infrastruktur]], which establishes how these corridors must be sized, positioned, and shared between utility providers and road authorities across Selangor's 12 local authorities.

## Design Basis by Road Width

Corridor requirements are differentiated by road Right-of-Way (R.O.W.) width, using two thresholds — roads ≥20.12m and roads <20.12m — following standards set jointly by [[entities/planmalaysia-selangor]] (2012) and [[entities/jabatan-kerja-raya-jkr]] Selangor (2022). This connects the utility corridor framework directly to the state's broader [[concepts/road-hierarchy-selangor]], since the class and reserve width of a road determines the space available for utility placement.

Key design elements include:
- Minimum road reserve widths tied to corridor provision
- **DURC** (Dedicated Utilities Road Crossing) provisions for utilities crossing beneath roads
- Pipe-bata (utility duct/sleeve) specifications for protected crossings

## Placement and Common Trenching

Utility corridors are required along both sides of roads, positioned outside the formal road R.O.W. Underground utility lines follow a **common trenching** concept — multiple utility types sharing a single excavated corridor in a defined stacking order, rather than each provider trenching independently. This reduces repeated road-cutting, lowers implementation and maintenance costs, and aligns with the resource-efficiency principles described in the source document.

Jadual KU1 (the corridor standards table) specifies minimum spacing/clearance distances (jarak susunan) for each utility type relative to:
- The road reserve boundary
- Other utility lines running in parallel
- The ground surface (depth of cover)

Cross-section diagrams in the source document (Rajah KU1(1)–(3)) illustrate corridor layouts within 66-foot and 100-foot R.O.W. road cross-sections, and the underground stacking order both along roadside utility lanes and at road crossings.

## Network Routing and River Coordination

Corridor alignment must consider:
- Land acquisition needed for new corridor routes
- Coordination with existing and planned road alignments
- Coordination with river and drainage reserve guidelines where utility corridors cross or run near watercourses, requiring sign-off from KUSEL and JPS (the drainage and irrigation authority)

## Development Incentives

To encourage developers to surrender land for utility corridor provision, the guideline links corridor land surrender to planning incentives, including a 10% open space allowance and a 3% affordable housing incentive — connecting utility corridor planning to [[concepts/affordable-housing-provision-standards]] as a mechanism for extracting public infrastructure land from private development.

## Relation to Other Infrastructure Standards

Utility corridors function as the shared physical space in which several other infrastructure systems are embedded, including electrical cabling governed by [[concepts/electrical-substation-siting]] and [[entities/tenaga-nasional-berhad-tnb]] design standards. Buffer and spacing requirements within the corridor also echo the setback logic seen in [[concepts/buffer-zone-requirements]] for other infrastructure types.
