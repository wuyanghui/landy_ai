# Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design (CPTED)

## Overview

Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design (CPTED) is a design-based crime-prevention approach embedded throughout the [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]] guideline (Section 3.6, Bahagian A) as a foundational policy underpinning the siting and layout standards for community facilities across Selangor. Rather than relying on enforcement alone, CPTED shapes the physical environment—buildings, landscaping, circulation, and lighting—to reduce opportunities for crime and increase perceived and actual safety.

## Four Universal Principles

The guideline sets out four universal CPTED principles, supported by three implementation strategies, which are then expanded into five numbered design elements applied across facility types:

1. **Natural Surveillance** — Ensuring clear sightlines and visibility (e.g., unobstructed views across pedestrian walkways, public markets, and open spaces) so that occupants and passersby can naturally observe activity and deter wrongdoing.
2. **Access Control** — Directing and limiting entry/exit points to a site or building to distinguish legitimate users from intruders, reinforced through gates, guard posts, and controlled vehicular/pedestrian routes.
3. **Territorial Reinforcement (Territoriality)** — Using physical cues (fencing, signage, landscaping, building orientation) to establish clear ownership and boundaries, signaling that a space is monitored and cared for.
4. **Maintenance and Management** — Keeping facilities well-maintained to signal active use and oversight, since neglected environments invite disorder and crime.

A fifth element, **Support Activity**, is also identified—encouraging land uses and programming that bring legitimate, continuous activity to a space, further discouraging criminal behavior through natural occupancy.

## Application Across the Guideline

CPTED is not confined to a single facility type but recurs as a cross-cutting design requirement across nearly every category (KM1–KM9) in Bahagian B:

- **General Standards (Section 6.0, Jadual KM(1))** — CPTED safety principles are listed alongside universal design/OKU accessibility provisions as mandatory considerations for all proposed community facility components, unless superseded by a facility-specific standard in Bahagian B.
- **Safety Facilities (KM3)** — CPTED is most explicitly operationalized here. Police headquarters (IPK/IPD, KM3-1) and police stations/beat bases (Balai Polis/Pondok Polis, KM3-2) incorporate CPTED-aligned siting criteria such as visibility from main roads, location within or near administrative/institutional centers, and building orientation toward roads and shared boundaries — paired with guard posts as physical access-control points. Fire and rescue stations (Balai Bomba dan Penyelamat, KM3-3) extend the same logic with watch towers for natural surveillance, guard posts for access control, and drill yards/logistics buildings sited for both operational readiness and territorial definition.
- **Basic Planning Principles (Section 5.0)** — CPTED is explicitly referenced under Principle 3, *Keselesaan dan Keselamatan* (Comfort and Safety), alongside Universal Design Guidelines, requiring safe visual surveillance across facility layouts, including Temporary Placement Centres used during disaster/pandemic response.
- **Markets and Public Realm (KM8)** — Although not labeled explicitly as CPTED, public market design standards echo natural surveillance and territoriality principles through requirements for clear visual screening of waste disposal areas away from public view, reinforcing the guideline's broader pattern of designing sightlines and boundaries into everyday facilities.
- Illustrative photographic examples in the source document (pedestrian walkways, public markets, and public spaces) demonstrate how natural surveillance and territorial reinforcement are realized in built form.

## Related Concepts

CPTED works in tandem with [[concepts/oku-accessibility-standards]] as one of the two universal, cross-facility design mandates in the guideline, and complements [[concepts/parking-standards]] and site layout requirements by shaping how circulation and sightlines are configured for safety. It also intersects with [[concepts/population-based-facility-hierarchy]], since larger, higher-catchment facilities (e.g., IPK vs. IPD police facilities, or state-level vs. community-level fire stations) apply CPTED principles at greater scale and complexity.
