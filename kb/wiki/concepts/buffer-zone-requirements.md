# Buffer Zone Requirements

## Overview

Buffer zones (Zon Penampan) are mandatory separation distances between land uses that pose pollution, noise, safety, or nuisance risks and their surrounding sensitive uses — primarily residential areas. The concept originates as a core planning principle in [[summaries/PEQ-Perindustrian]], tied to the classification of industry by pollution intensity, but recurs throughout Selangor's planning guidelines wherever a potentially hazardous or disruptive facility — industrial premises, electrical substations, transmission corridors, telecommunications towers, sewage treatment plants, or community facilities such as burial grounds, religious buildings, and safety installations — must be sited near housing or other sensitive land uses, as set out in [[summaries/PEQ-infrastruktur]] and [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]].

## Distance Standards by Industry Class

The guideline sets minimum buffer distances scaled to industrial intensity:

- **Light Industry (Industri Ringan)** — 50m minimum separation from residential areas. Light industry produces minimal pollution and can generally be sited closer to housing.
- **Medium Industry (Industri Sederhana)** — 150m minimum separation. Medium industry generates moderate waste, noise, and gas emissions, requiring greater distance from residential zones.
- **Heavy Industry (Industri Berat)** — 300m minimum separation. Heavy industry carries higher risks of fire, explosion, biological contamination, and raw material/waste pollution, demanding the largest buffer.

These thresholds derive from the eight foundational planning principles (Prinsip Asas Perancangan) in Bahagian A of the guideline, particularly the site-planning principle (zoning by pollution rate, site suitability, and safety) and the dedicated buffer-zone principle referencing recommendations from the Department of Environment (Jabatan Alam Sekitar).

## Buffer Zones in Infrastructure Planning

PEQ-infrastruktur applies the same buffer-zone logic to utility and infrastructure facilities, setting out its own set of minimum separation and reserve distances:

- **Electrical substations** ([[concepts/electrical-substation-siting]]) — Pencawang Masuk Utama (PMU) and Pencawang Pembahagian Utama (PPU) siting standards require a minimum 100m setback from sewerage treatment plants, along with flood-prone platform level and access road width conditions, subject to final approval by Tenaga Nasional Berhad ([[entities/tenaga-nasional-berhad-tnb]]).
- **TNB transmission corridors (Rentis TNB)** — Buildings must be set back a minimum of 30.48m (100ft) from transmission tower (pylon) legs, with no structures or pipelines permitted within this zone. Transmission line reserve widths scale by voltage: 20m per side (33kV/132kV/275kV lines) versus 25m per side (500kV lines), with additional forest-reserve buffers where applicable.
- **Telecommunications towers** — Minimum inter-tower spacing of 1km applies to towers exceeding 45m in height, with additional setback distances varying by surrounding land use (residential, commercial, industrial, institutional) and tower height category, and slope-stability conditions for towers sited near cut slopes or embankments.
- **Sewage treatment plants (Loji Rawatan Kumbahan)** — Buffer distances from residential premises vary by plant enclosure type: 20.12–30.48m (66–100ft) for open treatment plants, 10.06m (33ft) for fully enclosed plants, and no minimum buffer for buried/covered plants serving under 150 population equivalent (subject to odour and noise mitigation). Siting criteria also require distance from water sources, cemeteries, playgrounds, and food premises, and avoidance of flood-prone or erosion-prone land.

These infrastructure-specific buffers serve the same underlying purpose as industrial buffer zones — protecting residential and other sensitive uses from pollution, noise, and safety hazards — but are calibrated to the risk profile of each facility type rather than a single industry classification scale.

## Buffer Zones in Community Facility Planning

PEQ-K-Masyarakat (Garis Panduan dan Piawaian Perancangan Kemudahan Masyarakat Negeri Selangor, 2025) establishes both a general buffer standard and facility-specific setback rules for the ten community facility categories (KM1–KM9):

- **General perimeter planting standard (Jadual KM(1))** — a baseline rule applying to all community facility types unless overridden by a facility-specific standard in Bahagian B: minimum 3.048m (10ft) landscaped setback from main roads and 1.524m (5ft) from shared boundaries, alongside a 60% maximum plinth-area coverage cap that indirectly preserves setback and buffer land on every site.
- **Health facilities (KM1)** — hospitals and health clinics carry their own building setback, site-width, and access-road dimensions scaled to facility category (e.g., Hospital Negeri vs. Klinik Komuniti); clinic siting guidance also calls for early consultation to avoid overlapping routes and pollution exposure near main entrances.
- **Education (KM2)** — school and IPTA sites must be buffered from major roads, industrial areas, and high-voltage lines/pylons, with minimum front/side/rear setbacks and service-road widths set per facility tier; tadika/taska (kindergarten/nursery) sites require a minimum 6.10m (20ft) setback from front and side boundaries and are prohibited near quarries, tailings dams, abandoned mining pools, or contaminated land.
- **Religious facilities (KM4)** — mosques and surau require setback from noise/vibration sources such as electrical substations and transmission lines, and prohibit siting on former mining land or unstable ground without geological clearance. Non-Islamic places of worship (RISI) additionally require setback distance from other places of worship, exclude sites on slopes over 25°, and prohibit siting on sacred or heritage land.
- **Burial facilities (KM5)** — cemetery layouts require a minimum 1.5m landscaped buffer between burial rows/zones; columbaria require a minimum 1,000m separation from residential areas plus compliant land-use zoning; crematoria require a minimum 0.6ha site with dedicated setback/buffer distances plus perimeter planting, reflecting the same logic as [[entities/tabung-amanah-perkuburan-negeri-selangor]] contribution rules for burial land provision.
- **Safety facilities (KM3)** — fire and rescue stations must avoid environmentally sensitive areas (KSAS), ex-mining, and swamp land, with a minimum 75km spacing between stations; police headquarters and stations require siting at least 10–20 years' distance from projected fire/high-risk cargo growth zones and a minimum 20m building setback from main roads.
- **Public markets (KM8)** — waste disposal areas must be visually screened and landscaped away from public view, with separate holding areas for halal and non-halal waste, layered onto the standard road-frontage and shared-boundary setback dimensions (12.19m/40ft and 6.10m/20ft respectively).
- **Transport terminals (KM9)** — perimeter planting setbacks of 3.048m (10ft) along main roads and 1.524m (5ft) along shared boundaries, with a minimum terminal boundary wall height of 2.438m (8ft).

Across nearly all of these facility types, buffer and setback rules are paired with a geological site-suitability check — a Borang Pengesahan Geologi Tapak (BPGT) from [[entities/jabatan-mineral-dan-geosains-jmg]] — wherever a site falls within or near an Environmentally Sensitive Area (KSAS) or unstable/ex-mining ground; see [[concepts/geological-site-clearance-bpgt]].

## Permitted Buffer Zone Activities

Buffer zones are not simply empty setback land — the guidelines specify compatible uses within these zones, including:

- Green landscaping and planting
- Drainage infrastructure
- Utility corridors ([[concepts/utility-corridor-standards]]), such as electrical, water, and communication lines routed via common trenching

This allows buffer land to serve environmental and infrastructural functions rather than remaining unused space.

## Relationship to Other Standards

Buffer zone requirements interact with several other general standards across the guidelines:

- **Perimeter planting (penanaman tepian)** standards, which set minimum landscaped setback widths for standalone and terrace/link industrial buildings as well as for community facility sites, complement buffer zoning by adding a vegetative screening layer.
- **[[concepts/worker-welfare-facilities]]** — worker dormitories and amenities sited within or near industrial premises must also account for buffer separation from heavier industrial activity to protect worker safety and health.
- **[[concepts/sustainable-industrial-planning]]** — Eco-Industrial Park (EIP) practices, such as green design and pollution prevention zoning, are reinforced by buffer requirements, since well-managed buffer land can double as ecological or amenity space.
- **[[concepts/utility-corridor-standards]]** — utility corridor placement along road reserves and common-trenching arrangements are governed by their own minimum spacing tables, which parallel the buffer-distance logic applied to industrial and infrastructure siting.
- **[[concepts/population-based-facility-hierarchy]]** — community facility buffer and setback distances are typically layered on top of population-catchment site-area standards, so a larger-tier facility (e.g., a district hospital or district cemetery) generally carries both a larger minimum site area and a larger buffer requirement.

## Type-Specific Application

While buffer zone distances are set as general standards in Bahagian A of PEQ-Perindustrian, as facility-specific tables in PEQ-infrastruktur, and as both a general standard and facility-specific rules in PEQ-K-Masyarakat, they inform the setback, perimeter planting, and siting rules applied across type-specific guidelines — terrace, detached/cluster, strata, stratified, data centre, worker housing, and Managed Industrial Park developments; electrical substations, transmission corridors, telecommunications towers, and sewage treatment plants; and community facilities including schools, health clinics and hospitals, police and fire stations, mosques and surau, cemeteries and crematoria, community centres, libraries, public markets, and transport terminals — particularly wherever these facilities are located adjacent to or near residential land uses.
