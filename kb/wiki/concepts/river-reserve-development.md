# River Reserve Development Standards

## Overview

River Reserve Development Standards (AS5, *Garis Panduan dan Piawaian Pembangunan Atas Rizab Sungai*) set out how land within and adjacent to river reserves and river corridors may be developed in Selangor. Published in [[summaries/PEQ-alam-sekitar]], the standard is one of six special technical guidelines (AS1–AS6) that operationalize environmental sensitivity controls for specific landscape types, alongside coastal, lake, hillside, and tree-preservation rules.

## Purpose and Guiding Principles

The standard is built around three overarching goals:

- **Preserving river ecosystem function** — protecting water quality, flow, and riparian habitat from encroaching development.
- **Maintaining riparian buffers** — keeping a physical margin between built structures and the watercourse to allow flood conveyance, bank stability, and maintenance access.
- **Coordinated approval** — development proposals within river reserves require sign-off from multiple agencies, including [[entities/jabatan-pengairan-dan-saliran-jps]], [[entities/lembaga-urus-air-selangor-luas]], the local Pihak Berkuasa Tempatan (PBT), and the State Government (PBN).

## Zoning Structure

Development control is organized around a set of nested zones radiating from the watercourse:

- **River zone** — the channel and immediate bank, where only water-dependent structures (bridges, jetties) are permitted.
- **River corridor / control zone** — a managed buffer where limited, non-permanent, or waterfront-oriented uses are allowed.
- **Areas outside the river reserve** — where general land use planning parameters apply, subject to setback from the reserve boundary.

Reserve width itself is determined using JPS/LUAS/PLANMalaysia tables that key off river width category, and by channel type (natural, lined, embanked, or semi-natural), each with its own cross-section profile.

## Permitted and Prohibited Uses

**Permitted within the river reserve (subject to conditions):**
- Public promenades, pedestrian and cycling paths, jogging tracks
- Kiosks, seating, lighting, and telecom poles
- Non-permanent commercial structures — cafes, restaurants, floating markets, food trucks
- Landscape and recreation facilities — parks, playgrounds, sports courts, with tree preservation maintained
- Floating structures, boat landings, and water taxi facilities
- Utility crossings (pipes, bridges, cables) meeting minimum soffit level and erosion-control requirements

**Prohibited or restricted:**
- Permanent buildings such as workshops, hotels, or sewage treatment facilities
- Structures that obstruct flow or reduce flood conveyance capacity
- Riverfront housing without adequate setback from the reserve line

## Infrastructure and Crossing Standards

The guideline includes cross-section diagrams for public roads, catchment ponds, highways, railways, and bridges crossing or running alongside river reserves, specifying required setback distances between the reserve boundary and the control zone. Utility structures crossing rivers must meet minimum soffit clearance and include riverbank erosion-control measures.

## Relationship to Other Standards

River reserve controls sit alongside other environmentally sensitive area rules in the same document — hillside/highland development, lake preservation, and coastal setbacks — all of which apply comparable logic of tiered buffer zones and permitted-use tables keyed to proximity from a sensitive natural feature. See also [[concepts/buffer-zone-requirements]], [[concepts/kawasan-sensitif-alam-sekitar-ksas]], and [[concepts/utility-corridor-standards]] for related zoning and infrastructure-crossing principles used elsewhere in Selangor's planning framework. Enforcement and technical guidance draws on legal provisions consolidated under [[entities/akta-172]] and is informed by the state's broader environmental direction under [[entities/rsn-selangor-2035]].
