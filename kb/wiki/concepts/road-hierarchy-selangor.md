# Road Hierarchy in Selangor

## Overview

Road hierarchy is the foundational classification system used in Selangor's infrastructure planning to determine road design standards, reserve widths, access control, and traffic function. It is defined in the PL1 and PL2 sections of [[summaries/PEQ-infrastruktur]] and sourced from the [[entities/road-engineering-association-of-malaysia]] (2002) standard, subject to updates by [[entities/jabatan-kerja-raya-jkr]] and the local authorities (PBT). The same hierarchy underpins the internal and service road widths prescribed for individual community facility site layouts in [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]].

## Two Main Categories

Roads in Malaysia — and specifically in Selangor — are divided into two primary categories based on population density:

- **Luar Bandar (Rural)** — areas with population density below 10,000 people per sq km, classified R1–R6
- **Bandar (Urban)** — areas with population density above 10,000 people per sq km, classified U1–U6

Within each category, roads are ranked from Class 1 (highest function/speed) to Class 6 (lowest), covering:

1. Lebuhraya Ekspres (Expressway)
2. Jalan Arteri / Lebuhraya (Arterial Road / Highway)
3. Jalan Pengumpul / Jalan Utama (Collector Road / Primary Road)
4. Jalan Tempatan / Sekunder / Kecil (Local / Secondary / Minor Road)

Example rural expressways cited in the guideline include DASH, Lebuhraya Sungai Besi–Ulu Kelang (SUKE), and Lebuhraya Lembah Klang Selatan (SKVE).

## Design Standards by Class

A detailed cross-reference table (Piawaian Reka Bentuk Jalan Raya) sets design parameters for each class:

- **Design speed**: ranging from ≥40 km/j (lowest local roads) up to ≥90 km/j (expressways)
- **Access control**: none, partial, or full, depending on class
- **Traffic flow type and road function/geometry**: varies by hierarchy level

## Minimum Reserve Widths

PL2 provides minimum road width/reserve standards (in metres and feet) cross-referenced by:

- **Road type**: Lebuhraya Ekspres, Lebuhraya, Jalan Persekutuan (Federal Road), Jalan Negeri (State Road), Jalan Sekunder, Jalan Kecil, Jalan/Lorong Tepi, Jalan/Lorong Belakang
- **Network level**: National, State, Interchange/Roundabout, Junction, Urban, Support
- **Land use type**: Residential (including Perumahan Sebaris/single-row and Perumahan Dua Baris/two-row), Commercial, Industrial
- **Setting**: Urban vs Rural, and junction type (Bertingkat/grade-separated vs Searas/at-grade)

Classification notes define U1–U6 as urban roads within townships with population exceeding 10,000, and R1–R6 as rural roads outside PBT control areas or connecting areas beyond 5km from urban centres.

## Facility Site Access Roads (KM1–KM9)

The community facility standards in [[summaries/PEQ-K-Masyarakat]] translate the general road hierarchy into concrete site-access requirements, expressed as minimum internal road (jalan dalaman) and service road (jalan perkhidmatan) widths keyed to facility category and its position in the [[concepts/population-based-facility-hierarchy]]:

- **20.12m (66ft) service roads** — required at the largest, highest-catchment facilities: state/major hospitals, police contingent/district headquarters (IPK/IPD), transport terminals, and fire stations.
- **15.24m (50ft) internal roads** — the recurring standard for mid-tier facilities such as health clinics, police stations (Balai Polis), mosques, and public markets.
- **12.19m (40ft) frontage/access roads** — applied to smaller facilities and building-frontage requirements, including suraus, non-Islamic places of worship, and market driveways/aisles.
- **6.10m (20ft) shared-boundary roads** — the minimum applied at shared lot boundaries across most facility categories.

These tiers mirror the same collector/local road logic used in PL1/PL2, scaled to facility size rather than general land use, and are consistently paired with [[concepts/buffer-zone-requirements]] (e.g., 3.048m/10ft perimeter planting along main roads, 1.524m/5ft at shared boundaries) and geometric setbacks that echo the urban/rural (U/R) distinctions in the base hierarchy.

## Relationship to Other Standards

Road hierarchy is the base layer that other infrastructure standards build upon:

- **[[concepts/parking-standards]]** — minimum road/lane widths for parking access (e.g., TLK sections) are set relative to the adjoining road's hierarchy class and land use, and facility-specific parking ratios in KM1–KM9 assume adjoining roads meet these minimum widths
- **[[concepts/utility-corridor-standards]]** — utility corridor placement and reserve sizing depend on the road's Right-of-Way (R.O.W.) width, itself derived from hierarchy classification
- Pedestrian, cycling, and micromobility lane provisions (special lanes for bicycles and e-scooters) are layered onto the road cross-section according to hierarchy and reserve width

## Policy Context

The road hierarchy framework supports broader state and national transport goals, including the connectivity thrusts of [[entities/rmk12]] and [[entities/rfn4]], the [[entities/rsn-selangor-2035]] structure plan's transportation measures, and safety objectives under the [[entities/pkjr-road-safety-plan]].
